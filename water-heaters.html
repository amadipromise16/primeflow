Abilene Water Hardness Alert: 240 mg/L Calcium Carbonate (14 Grains/Gal)

Abilene groundwater averages around 240 milligrams per liter calcium carbonate, which works out to roughly 14 grains per gallon. That is firmly in the hard range, and it comes from the limestone and gypsum formations that Abilene's reservoir and groundwater sources move through before reaching your tap.

Inside a tank water heater, that hardness becomes a physical problem. As water is heated, dissolved calcium and magnesium fall out of solution and settle to the bottom of the tank as a crust of scale and sediment. That sediment bed insulates the burner from the water above it, so the unit burns more fuel to deliver the same amount of hot water. Trapped water under the sediment boils and escapes in bursts, producing the popping and rumbling many Abilene homeowners hear. The overheated tank bottom also fatigues the steel and glass lining, causing tanks to fail years earlier than expected.

    WHAT DOES A WATER HEATER DIAGNOSTIC INVOLVE?

    We check the burner or heating elements, thermostat, gas valve or wiring, anode rod, and connections, and we inspect the tank for sediment and corrosion. On gas units we also confirm safe venting. That full check lets us pinpoint the failure instead of guessing, so you only pay for what actually needs fixing.

    SHOULD I CHOOSE A TANKLESS WATER HEATER INSTEAD OF A TANK?

    Tankless water heaters provide continuous hot water on demand and take up virtually zero floor space by mounting cleanly against a wall. Because they don't store 50 gallons of sitting water, there is no large tank reservoir to rust out from Abilene's hard water. However, tankless units require larger gas lines and dedicated venting. During our free in-home estimate, our technicians evaluate your household water usage, gas meter capacity, and electrical panel to explain whether a high-efficiency tank or tankless system makes the most financial sense for your family.
